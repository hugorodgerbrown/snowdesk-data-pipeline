{% load i18n components %} {% comment %} templates/includes/_hourly_chart.html — one day's weather as a meteogram. Replaces the 24-row hourly table (_forecast_hourly_body.html) with a chart: temperature and freezing level as lines, new snow and precipitation as bars, wind speed and gusts beneath, all on one shared axis. Context: chart : HourlyChart — every coordinate, already formatted, from apps.weather.services.hourly_chart.build_hourly_chart. testid_prefix : str — data-testid namespace (default "hourly-chart"). about : dict — optional legend metadata rows: elevation, freezing, updated, source. Absent rows don't render. Renders nothing when ``chart`` is absent — a day with no hourly series gets no empty chart frame. The caller tests for it. Four things about this template are load-bearing: 1. **Three charts, one x-axis.** Temperature (hourly), precipitation (hourly) and wind (three-hourly) each have their own header, summary and vertical scale; they share the time axis and nothing else. Within that, the axis ticks and labels mark clock INSTANTS while the wind's three-hour aggregates sit at the CENTRE of the span they cover, so the aggregates land between ticks rather than on them. The service keeps all of it on one x-scale; see its docstring. 2. **Every number is HTML, not .** Text inside a viewBox scales with the drawing, so an axis label sized to look right on a desktop card is illegible on a phone and vice versa. All labels are absolutely positioned HTML over the SVG, at a fixed px font-size, placed by the percentages the service emits — so they track the scaling while their size does not. 3. **Every coordinate arrives as a string.** Django localises floats on output, so a bare float would render x="12,5" under a comma-decimal locale and the whole drawing would vanish with no error. The service formats in Python, which puts that beyond reach here. 4. **Wind arrows point at the SOURCE** — where the weather is coming from, which is what the legend says and what loads a slope. Note this is the opposite of the ``wind_arrow_rotation`` filter used by _weather_panel and _forecast_panel, which points downwind; see the service's docstring. {% comment %} The time now, as a pin that CROSSES the bar. TWO TONES, AND THAT IS NOT DECORATION. No single flat colour can clear 3:1 against both fills in light mode, and the arithmetic says so rather than the eye: to separate from slate-600 an ink needs a relative luminance of at least 0.358, while to separate from amber-500 it needs either 1.54 — brighter than white — or at most 0.127. There is no number satisfying both. So the pin is a text-1 core inside a card-coloured outline, and whichever fill it crosses, one of the two carries it: on the lit segment the core reads at 8.2:1, on the night ground the outline reads at 7.6:1, and the dark theme mirrors it at 10.2:1 and 4.0:1. The two are 17:1 apart from each other, so they still read as one mark rather than two.
