{% load i18n l10n %} {% comment %} templates/includes/_forecast_hourly_chart.html — one day's meteogram. Rendered by ``_forecast_panel.html`` for each day that carries an hourly series, beneath the day strip that selects it. Replaces the six-column hour table (``_forecast_hourly_body.html``, SNOW-776): twenty-four rows of numbers is a data dump, and the shape of the day — when it warms, when it snows, when it blows — is what a reader is actually after. Three bands on one 24-hour x axis: A temperature bars drawn from the ZERO ISOTHERM, so a below-zero day reads as below zero, with the freezing-level height laid over them on its own scale (metres, not °C). B precipitation hanging from the top of its band — rain falls. An hour that is snowing takes the snow token instead of the rain one. C wind speed with gusts above it and the gap between them shaded. The gap is the gustiness, which is the point of drawing both. **No JavaScript.** Every coordinate is computed by ``apps.weather.services.weather_display.build_hourly_chart`` and arrives here as SVG user units, so the chart draws under the service worker offline and its arithmetic is covered by pytest. ``{% templatetag openblock %} localize off {% templatetag closeblock %}`` wraps the whole drawing and is load-bearing: coordinates are floats, and an active locale that formats decimals with a comma would otherwise render ``x="12,5"`` and take every band out. Colours are ``--color-chart-*`` design tokens surfaced as ``fill-*`` / ``stroke-*`` utilities, so the SVG follows the theme and carries no hex.
