{% load i18n %} {% comment %} templates/includes/_hourly_chart_legend.html — the chart's key. Opened by the info button in _hourly_chart.html's header. Names every mark the chart draws, states the two things a reader can get wrong about a freezing level, and gives the forecast's own provenance. Context: prefix : str — data-testid namespace, passed down by the chart. about : dict — optional provenance rows: ``elevation``, ``freezing``, ``updated``, ``source``. Each row renders only when present. **The swatches are drawn with the same stroke, dash and fill as the series they explain** — same tokens, same widths, same dash arrays. A legend drawn in approximations is worse than none, because it teaches the reader a mark that is not on the chart. A plain in-card dialog rather than an overlay-registry sheet: every surface this chart is going on is an off-map page, and the map's mutual-exclusion registry exists to stop two *map* overlays being open at once. Registering here would couple a weather component to the map for no behaviour. If the chart later lands inside a map popup, that is the moment to revisit it. {% endcomment %} {% firstof prefix "hourly-chart" as ns %}
{% comment %} Drag handle. Decorative on a pointer, but it is what makes the panel read as a sheet on a phone, which is the treatment the design asks for at that width. {% endcomment %}

{% comment %} The three rules. They are not series, and the chart distinguishes them by stroke: 0 °C is the one FIXED value on the drawing and is dashed; the station's elevation and the current time are both VARIABLES and share a solid hairline. Naming all three here is what makes that convention readable rather than decorative. Each row also states the condition under which the rule appears at all, because all three are conditional and a reader who does not see one should know whether it is missing or simply not applicable. {% endcomment %}
