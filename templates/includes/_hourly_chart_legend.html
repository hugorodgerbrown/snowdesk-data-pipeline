{% load i18n %} {% comment %} templates/includes/_hourly_chart_legend.html — the chart's key. Opened by the info button in _hourly_chart.html's header. Names the nine marks a reader cannot place unaided, warns about the one thing a freezing level is routinely mistaken for, and gives the forecast's own provenance. IT IS A KEY, NOT A GLOSSARY (SNOW-790). One bare label per row, and on anything wider than a phone a card in the corner rather than a band across the whole width. It is read WITH the chart in view: a panel big enough to explain itself hides what it is explaining, which is how ten rows of prose across the full width came to cover the entire drawing. Below sm: it stays a full-width bottom sheet with a drag handle, because at a phone's width there is no corner to sit in and the sheet is the treatment the design asks for. Context: prefix : str — data-testid namespace, passed down by the chart. about : dict — optional provenance rows: ``elevation``, ``freezing``, ``updated``, ``source``. Each row renders only when present. **The swatches are drawn with the same stroke, dash and fill as the series they explain** — same tokens, same widths, same dash arrays. A legend drawn in approximations is worse than none, because it teaches the reader a mark that is not on the chart. A plain in-card dialog rather than an overlay-registry sheet: every surface this chart is going on is an off-map page, and the map's mutual-exclusion registry exists to stop two *map* overlays being open at once. Registering here would couple a weather component to the map for no behaviour. If the chart later lands inside a map popup, that is the moment to revisit it. {% endcomment %} {% firstof prefix "hourly-chart" as ns %}

{% comment %} Nine rows, one bare label each (SNOW-790). Every row used to carry a sentence — what a freezing level is, when a rule is drawn, which hour gets the figure — and ten sentences made a sheet tall enough to cover the chart it was explaining. A key is read while looking at the mark it names, so it has to leave the mark visible. One mark is deliberately NOT listed: the dashed 0 °C rule, which meets a labelled °C axis at zero and says what it is. The key is for marks a reader cannot place, and that is not one. The daylight bar IS listed, at the end, even though it sits under the hours it describes — a two-tone bar is a new mark on this chart and naming it costs one row. The times themselves are not here; they are spoken in the chart's aria-label (see ``_aria_label`` in the service). Note that the "About this forecast" block below is supplied by the component library alone, so it is not a place anything load-bearing can live. {% endcomment %}
