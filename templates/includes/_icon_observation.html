{% comment %} templates/includes/_icon_observation.html — the field-observation glyph: a pair of binoculars (SNOW-333 drew a warning triangle; extracted here by SNOW-658 and redrawn on Hugo's request). Hugo asked for Font Awesome's ``fa-regular fa-binoculars``. Font Awesome is not a dependency of this project — every icon here is an inline SVG, and the only two mentions of it in the tree are CC BY 4.0 attribution comments in static/js/map.js for paths traced from the Free set. Adding the library for one glyph would also not have got that exact class: binoculars in the REGULAR style is Font Awesome Pro (Free carries it in solid only). This is the same mark drawn in the stroke style every other icon in this project uses, so it sits correctly beside the star and the viewfinder. Why binoculars rather than the warning triangle it replaces: the triangle said "hazard", which is the bulletin's job — the danger scale, the choropleth, the problem cards all carry it. A field observation is a report of something SEEN, and the panel is the user's own list of what they have looked at and logged. The triangle also collided with the avalanche-problem iconography elsewhere on the page. One source for one mark. The glyph identifies the field-observation roundel (#report-btn, public/partials/_map_embed.html) AND, at 22px, the header of the panel that roundel opens — Hugo's design makes the roundel's own icon the panel's only identity mark, so a reader can confirm which roundel they tapped.
