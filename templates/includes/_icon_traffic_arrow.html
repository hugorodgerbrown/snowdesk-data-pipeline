{% comment %} templates/includes/_icon_traffic_arrow.html — the network-traffic glyph: a plain arrow, drawn pointing UP (SNOW-921). One mark for both directions. ``includes/nav.html``'s network-menu trigger includes it twice — once as it is drawn for "a request went out", once with ``svg_class="rotate-180"`` for "a response came back" — which is exactly the contract ``includes/_icon_chevron.html`` already has with the row disclosure: one source, rotated by its caller, so the two orientations cannot drift into two different arrows. A plain shaft-and-head arrow, deliberately NOT ``includes/_icon_download_arrow.html``. That mark ends on a baseline, because it means "fetch this onto the device" — a completed, deliberate act. This one means "something is moving in this direction, right now", and a line under it would read as an arrival every time the glyph lit. Sized small by default (12px, against the 15px the connectivity symbol beside it uses): the pair is ambient, sitting at low opacity until traffic lights it, and it must not compete with the mark that carries the actual connection state. The stroke is the house 2 on the house 24 grid, which resolves to ~1px at this size — thin on purpose, since the arrows spend almost all their time dimmed (src/css/main.css, ``[data-traffic-arrow]``).
