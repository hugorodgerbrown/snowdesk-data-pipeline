{% load i18n components %} {% comment %} trips/partials/_trip_card.html — one upcoming trip on the agenda (SNOW-823, redrawn as a card by SNOW-848). A CARD, NOT A ROW, and that is the change. The row this replaced put the day, a label and a distance on three cramped lines and left everything a person actually decides on — how big the day is, where the group meets — off the page. The card carries the whole answer: when, what, the organiser's note, the three figures, and the meeting point. The day and time still LEAD, because an agenda is read by when; the title answers "which one" only after the reader has found the right morning. THE TITLE IS THE LINK, and it is text-1 rather than accent. A card whose every line is clickable teaches nothing about what is clickable; one semantic link, on the thing being opened, does. WHAT LEFT IN SNOW-848. The "You organised this" chip and the "N people going" line are both gone, along with the roster surface they belonged to. A trip is a shareable item, not a social event: someone plans a tour, sends the link, and whoever opens it saves it to their own trips. Nobody sees who else is on it or how many. The data model is untouched — ``TripParticipant`` still records every save — this is what the page says about it. THE FOOTER'S RIGHT SLOT IS THE SHARE CONTROL, for a trip this reader organised. The design draws the footer as a space-between row with the meeting point alone on the left, and sharing is the entire journey a trip exists for — the organiser plans one and sends the link — so the slot the layout reserves is where that control belongs. Minting is organiser-scoped in ``mint_trip_share``, so a card for somebody else's trip carries no button at all rather than a disabled one: the site's "show controls, don't hide them" rule is about a control the reader could earn the right to press, and this is not one. SHARING AGAIN ROTATES THE LINK, which stops one already sent to a group chat working. The label says which press this is — "Share" for a trip with no live link, "Share again" for one that has — and the consequence rides in the button's title. When it is this trip, the card is marked as the one just written, so the confirmation toast has something to point at on an agenda with several trips on it. Compared as objects: two model instances with the same primary key are equal, and a None never matches, so no guard is needed around the test. {% endcomment %}
