{% trans "Data sources and licences" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Snowdesk renders avalanche bulletins published by three national and regional avalanche warning services. Each service licenses its own data, and the licence that applies to a bulletin is the one belonging to the service that issued it. {% endblocktrans %}

  • {% blocktrans trimmed %} WSL Institute for Snow and Avalanche Research SLF — bulletins for Switzerland, licensed under CC BY 4.0. {% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans trimmed %} Avalanche.report (ALBINA / EUREGIO) — bulletins for Tyrol, South Tyrol and Trentino, licensed under CC BY 4.0. {% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans trimmed %} Météo-France — avalanche bulletins (BRA) for the French mountain ranges, used under the Météo-France open-data licence. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "A complete rendering, not the authority" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Snowdesk aims to render each bulletin in full — every danger rating, avalanche problem and comment the issuing service published. Nothing is withheld or summarised away. It remains an information service: the avalanche warning service that issued the bulletin is the authority on it, and every bulletin page links back to the official source so you can check it for yourself. Snowdesk does not replace that source, professional avalanche training, or your own assessment of conditions in the field. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Always check the official bulletin and make your own on-site assessment" %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Avalanche conditions change rapidly and vary with aspect, elevation, and recent weather. No digital service can capture every local hazard. Before entering avalanche terrain, check the official bulletin for the region you are heading to — the European services are indexed at avalanches.org — seek current local knowledge, and make your own on-site assessment. Your safety is your responsibility. {% endblocktrans %}
