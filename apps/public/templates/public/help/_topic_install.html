{% blocktrans trimmed %} Snowdesk can be installed as an app on your phone or computer. Look for an "Install" or "Add to Home Screen" option in your browser's address bar or share menu — once installed, it opens in its own window without any browser chrome. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Pages you have already visited load instantly on repeat visits, and keep working with no connection, because both the app and the data behind those pages are kept on your device. Snowdesk always asks the network first and only falls back to what it holds. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} That means a rating you see offline is the last one this device fetched, not necessarily the one in force. The signal symbol at the top of every page tells you which it is: struck through whenever the connection is failing, or has failed often enough that Snowdesk has stopped trying and is serving stored data only. Tap it for how long ago this device last reached the server. Check that before you trust a rating, and reload once you have signal. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} You can also stop Snowdesk using the network yourself. If you are signed in, open the account menu and turn on "Offline mode" — before you lose coverage, or to spare a metered or roaming connection — and the app runs from what it has already stored. It stays on until you turn it off again: Snowdesk will not quietly reconnect. While it is on, the signal symbol at the top of every page is struck through, much like aeroplane mode on a phone, and tapping it tells you how old the stored data is and offers the way back. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The background map is the exception: it is stored only for the areas you download yourself — see "Offline downloads" above. {% endblocktrans %}
