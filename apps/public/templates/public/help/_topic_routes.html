{% blocktrans trimmed %} Routes are your own tracks, drawn on the map beside the avalanche picture. Sign in, open the routes button on the right of the map, and upload a .gpx file containing a track or a route — the kind of file a GPS watch, a planning tool or a touring app exports. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The panel lists everything you have uploaded, each row with its distance and, where the file recorded heights, its ascent and descent. You can rename a route in place or remove it, and the "Display on the map" switch at the foot draws them all as lines. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Tapping a line on the map opens the route's own popup: its name, the same figures, and an elevation profile of the whole track. The profile is drawn from the heights in your file, so a track recorded without them shows none rather than a flat line. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Routes are private. They are visible only to you, they are never shown to other users, and they play no part in the community-reports overlay. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Uploading needs a connection, as do renaming and removing — those actions are not queued for later the way a field observation is. There is a limit on the size of a single file and on how many routes one account can keep; the panel says so when you reach either. A very long track is thinned before it is stored, which keeps it quick to draw and leaves its shape, distance and profile intact. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Once your routes have loaded on a device, the lines stay on the map with no connection. The list inside the panel needs one, and says so instead of pretending you have no routes. {% endblocktrans %}
