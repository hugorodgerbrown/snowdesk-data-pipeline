{% blocktrans trimmed %} Every bulletin carries a danger rating on the five-level European scale — Low, Moderate, Considerable, High and Very High. The number and its colour are the headline: they say how likely avalanches are and how serious they would be, for the region as a whole. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The panel below the header breaks the day into windows. Most days have a single, unlabelled row: that rating holds all day and there is nothing more to say. When a provider expects conditions to change — a storm arriving, or the sun working on the snow — a second row appears with its own rating and a "Later" tag, as above. Only a window that departs from the baseline is tagged, so a tag on the panel always means the day is going somewhere. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Some providers split a window by height instead, giving one rating above a pivot elevation and another below it. Those rows carry a small mountain glyph shaded to show which half of the mountain they describe. {% endblocktrans %}
