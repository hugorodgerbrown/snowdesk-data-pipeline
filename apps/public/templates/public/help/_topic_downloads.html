{% comment %} apps/public/templates/public/help/_topic_downloads.html — "Offline downloads" panel body. Content partial for the help page; included via the body_template parameter of includes/_collapsible_panel.html. Documents the downloads roundel and its sheet (public/partials/_map_custom_download_control.html, _map_downloads_sheet.html, SNOW-522/588/634/635/645) plus the per-region download button in the ribbon header (_map_download_control.html), none of which had a help section. The distinction this panel exists to make is which of the two things a download is: it stores BASEMAP TILES for an area. Pages and avalanche data are cached separately, by visiting them — that half is the "Install & offline" panel's subject, and each panel points at the other rather than restating it. SNOW-749 adds a second distinction: the AREA (which region, or which framed box) follows the account, while the MAP DATA stays on the device that fetched it. The three paragraphs below exist to keep those apart, because the failure they prevent is a user seeing an area listed on a new phone and assuming the map is there too. They also carry the sign-in requirement, which is the one thing about downloads that changed for a signed-out reader. These were briefly gated on a `download_sync` waffle flag, alongside the feature itself. The flag was dropped before merge on query cost (see apps.public.views._downloads_context), so there is no longer a state in which the superseded "they do not follow your account to your phone" paragraph is true, and it is gone rather than kept in an else branch. {% endcomment %} {% load i18n %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A download saves the background map for an area onto this device, so the map still draws where there is no signal. The framed-square button on the right of the map opens "Your downloads": everything you have stored, what it is costing you, and the way to add more. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} There are two ways to store an area. Select a region on the map and use the download button beside its name in the header to take the whole region; or use "Download a custom area" inside the sheet, which lets you frame any part of the map yourself — the right choice for a tour that straddles a boundary. The sheet lists the two kinds separately, and custom areas can be renamed. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A budget at the top of the sheet caps how much space downloads may take, and you choose the figure. When a new download would take you past it, the oldest areas are removed to make room and you are asked first. Lowering the budget below what you already hold puts you over it, and the sheet says so rather than deleting anything behind your back. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Starting a download needs an account. The controls stay where they are when you are signed out and take you to the sign-in page instead; anything you have already downloaded keeps working exactly as before, signed in or not, because reading a stored map needs no connection and no account. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Your areas follow your account, but the map data does not. An area you save on a laptop is listed on your phone with a "Download here" button, and the space it takes is counted only on the device that actually holds it — so a listed area is not the same thing as an available one. The sheet dims the ones that are not on this device and shows no size for them. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The map data itself lives in this browser on this device. Clearing the site's data or resetting local data removes it, and the sheet gives you two ways to remove one yourself: "free up space" takes an area off this device and leaves it on your account, so it is one tap to bring back, while the bin removes it everywhere. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The "Display on the map" switch at the foot of the sheet shades the areas you hold, so you can see the edges of your own coverage before you set off. It is the only thing that turns that shading off again. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} An area is downloaded for the base map that was showing at the time. Switching to a different base map is switching to a map you have not stored, and the sheet offers to download the same area again for it. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Downloading needs a connection; listing and removing what you already hold works offline, which is when space usually runs short. A download can also fail because the device is out of room or the area is larger than the budget allows — frame a smaller area and try again. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A download stores the map, not the avalanche bulletins. Bulletin pages and the danger colours are kept for offline use as you visit them — see "Install & offline" below. {% endblocktrans %}
