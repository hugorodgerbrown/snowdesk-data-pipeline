The distinction this panel exists to make is what a download holds: the BASEMAP TILES for an area, and (since SNOW-924) the bulletins and weather inside its boundary. Other pages are cached separately, by visiting them — that half is the "Install & offline" panel's subject, and each panel points at the other rather than restating it. SNOW-832: the copy tracks the sheet's own regrouping — the list is headed by BASE MAP now, not by kind, so "lists the two kinds separately" became false, and "two ways to remove one" went with the "free up space" control that ticket removed. SNOW-1137: the sheet is grouped "On this device" / "Not on this device" now, not by basemap, so the per-basemap-heading sentence went; drop zones make it three ways to store an area; a row's actions live in its "…" menu; and "a download stores the map, not the bulletins" stopped being true with SNOW-924. SNOW-749 adds a second distinction: the AREA (which region, or which framed box) follows the account, while the MAP DATA stays on the device that fetched it. The three paragraphs below exist to keep those apart, because the failure they prevent is a user seeing an area listed on a new phone and assuming the map is there too. They also carry the sign-in requirement, which is the one thing about downloads that changed for a signed-out reader. These were briefly gated on a `download_sync` waffle flag, alongside the feature itself. The flag was dropped before merge on query cost (see apps.public.views._downloads_context), so there is no longer a state in which the superseded "they do not follow your account to your phone" paragraph is true, and it is gone rather than kept in an else branch. {% endcomment %} {% load i18n %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A download saves an area onto this device — the background map, and the avalanche bulletins and weather inside it — so it still works where there is no signal. The framed-square button on the right of the map opens "Your downloads": everything you have stored, the space it takes, and the way to add more. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} There are three ways to store an area. Select a region on the map and use the download button beside its name in the header to take the whole region. Use "Download a custom area" inside the sheet to frame any part of the map yourself — the right choice for a tour that straddles a boundary. Or tap the locate button and, once it has found you, tap it again to drop a zone: a circle of map around where you are standing, sized by zooming in or out. Each row in the sheet says which kind it is, and custom areas and drop zones can be renamed. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A budget at the top of the sheet caps how much space downloads may take, and you choose the figure. When a new download would take you past it, the oldest areas are removed to make room and you are asked first. Lowering the budget below what you already hold puts you over it, and the sheet says so rather than deleting anything behind your back. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Starting a download needs an account. The controls stay where they are when you are signed out and take you to the sign-in page instead; anything you have already downloaded keeps working exactly as before, signed in or not, because reading a stored map needs no connection and no account. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Your areas follow your account, but the downloaded maps do not. The sheet lists what you hold in two groups, "On this device" and "Not on this device". An area you saved on a laptop is listed on your phone under "Not on this device", dimmed and with no size, because the space it takes is counted only on the device that actually holds it — a listed area is not the same thing as an available one. "Download here", in that row's "…" menu, fetches the same area onto the phone. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The downloaded maps live in this browser on this device. Clearing the site's data or resetting local data removes them, and Remove, in a row's "…" menu, removes that download from this device only. The area stays in your list under "Not on this device", so you can download it again. To take it off your account, use Forget on that row; a device that still has the area downloaded will add it back. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} "Display downloaded areas", under Basemap in the layers menu — or the "Show on the map" switch at the foot of the downloads panel, which is the same setting — shades the areas you hold, so you can see the edges of your own coverage before you set off. Left alone it switches itself on whenever you are offline, which is when the answer matters; set it either way and your choice stands. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} An area is downloaded for the basemap that was showing at the time, and each row names it. Switching to a different basemap is switching to a map you have not stored. A region holds one basemap at a time: its download button then offers to replace the stored copy with this basemap's, and asks before it does. A custom area or drop zone you save again under another basemap is a second, separate download. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Downloading needs a connection; listing and removing what you already hold works offline, which is when space usually runs short. A download can also fail because the device is out of room or the area is larger than the budget allows — remove other downloads or raise the budget, or, for a custom area or drop zone, make it smaller and try again. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A download stores the avalanche bulletins and the weather for the ground inside it as well as the map, and "Sync now" in a row's "…" menu brings them up to date before you set off. Pages outside your downloads are kept for offline use as you visit them — see "Install & offline" below. {% endblocktrans %}
