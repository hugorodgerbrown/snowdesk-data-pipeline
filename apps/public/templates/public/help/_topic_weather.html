Content partial for the help page; included via the body_template parameter of includes/_collapsible_panel.html. Covers the SERVER-RENDERED weather — the row on a bulletin masthead, the per-location blocks on a resort page, the section on a saved pin's card. The map's Weather overlay is a different surface with different rules and has its own topic below (_topic_weather_layer.html). Rewritten for SNOW-761 rather than restored. The pre-SNOW-762 version of this topic described a coloured band whose hue and icon shifted between a day and a night palette; that band is gone, so a reader would have gone looking for something that is not there. {% endcomment %} {% load i18n %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Bulletin, resort and saved-pin pages carry the day's weather: a condition icon and label, the high and low temperature, any new snow, the freezing level, and when the sun rises and sets. It comes from Open-Meteo, not from the avalanche service — the bulletin's own weather description, written by the forecaster, is further down the bulletin page under "Snowpack & Weather". {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} A resort page shows one block per place we hold for it — typically the village, a mid-mountain station and the top — each labelled with its height. That is the point of showing more than one: rain in the village and snow on the summit are the same forecast read at two altitudes, and the difference is usually what decides the day. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Below the day itself is the week ahead: one column per day with its icon, temperatures and freezing level. The first few days open into an hour-by-hour breakdown — temperature, wind, gusts, precipitation and freezing level — and the later ones do not, because the forecast is not published at that resolution that far out. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Past dates show no weather at all. We record what the forecast said on each day going forward, so there is nothing on file for days before we started keeping it. A bulletin page for last winter shows the bulletin and no weather panel, rather than guessing. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The icon takes its day or night form from the time you are reading at, matched against sunrise and sunset where the place actually is — so a page read at midday looks like daytime whichever date you have navigated to. {% endblocktrans %}
