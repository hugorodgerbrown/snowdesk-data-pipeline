The first long-form help article, and THE TEMPLATE FOR THE ONES THAT FOLLOW: copy its skeleton, not its words. Companion to the FAQ panel at public/help/_topic_routes.html, which stays the short answer; this page is the walkthrough. Every claim here has to agree with that panel, and the panel's own copy is untouched apart from the one link it now carries here. THE SKELETON. A title and a one-line lead; then sections, each a real

over at most a few short paragraphs, a list, or an illustration; then the link back to /help/. `legal-prose` is the site's long-form document style (the colophon, the terms) — it sizes the section prose and the h2, so a section carries no class of its own. `max-w-narrow` keeps the measure readable; the page shell's default width is for pages with panels in them. WHAT GOES WHERE. Prose for the shape of a thing; numbered steps (includes/_help_steps.html) for something done in order; bullets (the same partial, `unordered`) for facts with no order; a definition list for a row of controls, each named and given one line. One illustration per surface the reader has to recognise, through includes/_help_illustration.html — a live mock of the real partial, never a screenshot (docs/decisions/help-illustrations-are-live-mocks.md). The illustration is inert and hidden from assistive technology, so the prose beside it has to stand alone: nothing here says "as shown". LENGTH. Around three hundred words. The first draft was twice that and read as a wall; what it lost was repetition of the FAQ panel, not facts. Brief: "what they are, how they work, where to find them". "What they are" is the lead line. "Where to find them" comes before "how they work" because the steps start by tapping a control the reader has to be able to find first. Two things this page carries that the panel does not, both verified against the running app rather than the templates: * The routes button's accessible name is "Your routes", not "Routes" — #route-add-btn in public/partials/_map_embed.html. * /account/routes/ is view-and-manage only. It renders includes/_ugc_panel_row.html rather than includes/_ugc_panel.html, so it has no "Add a route" control: uploading happens from the map panel alone. A reader who goes to the Routes page to add their first route finds nothing to press, which is why this page says where uploading lives. The routes roundel itself is not illustrated: its circle is styled from static/css/map.css, which this page does not load. The panel's header carries the same glyph, which is how the reader matches one to the other.
