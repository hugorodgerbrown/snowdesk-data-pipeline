It answers the question a reader asks of the toggle — "what does the date / terrain switch do?" — in the words the toggle uses: what Terrain takes away, what it leaves, and that the day is kept. {% endcomment %} {% load i18n %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} The switch at the start of the date row puts the map in one of two modes. The date half is the map for a day: the avalanche bulletin, the weather and the field reports, all for the date it shows. The mountain half is Terrain: the map for planning and browsing, with nothing on it that changes from one day to the next. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Terrain takes the day's layers off the map: the danger colours and the avalanche regions they are drawn in, the bulletin's problem slopes, the weather symbols, the field reports and the season timeline. Their rows in the layers menu are greyed out while you are in Terrain. It switches nothing on. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} Everything else stays as you set it, in both modes: Steep terrain, your routes, resorts, ski tours, wildlife rest zones and your pins. Turning one on or off in Terrain changes that layer alone. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans trimmed %} In Terrain the date shrinks to a calendar icon. Tap it to come back to the dated map, on the day you left, with each of the day's layers just as you had it. The map remembers which mode you were in, and a link you share opens in the same mode. {% endblocktrans %}
