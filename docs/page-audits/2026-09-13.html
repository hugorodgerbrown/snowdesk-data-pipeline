The unsent-bulletin-email lie — four audits running — is dead, on all 22 surfaces

SNOW-707, SNOW-875, SNOW-805, SNOW-808

This series flagged the same underlying falsehood in the first audit (11 August) and every one since: Snowdesk told readers, in various forms, that it emails them when a bulletin changes. Nothing ever did. The fix landed as a chain: SNOW-875 deleted the dead email-subscription machinery (both send functions, the unsubscribe tokens, two views, two routes, four templates); SNOW-805 dropped the Subscription table; SNOW-707 — filed 21 August naming ten surfaces, cancelled 27 minutes later, reopened 7 September the moment its cancellation's own reasoning (that SNOW-7 would eventually make the claims true) stopped holding — rewrote the copy. Its rescope swept the whole template tree with a grep rather than trusting the original list, and found 22 surfaces, not ten, including privacy.html:154's GDPR lawful-basis claim (a compliance defect, not marketing copy) and a live transactional email template. docs/user-journeys.md (SNOW-808) was rewritten the same week and now states plainly, in J4: "No scheduled job sends a bulletin — every email is transactional."

Consequences

Four things this week's work changes about the older tickets

Connections between this cycle's decisions and tickets written before them — visible from neither side alone.

A tracked finding can now disappear without being fixed or explained

SNOW-669, SNOW-674 and SNOW-678 were all cancelled on the same day, 7 September. SNOW-669 got a comment explaining exactly why the ticket could no longer be actioned as written, and the page audit that flagged it named by title. SNOW-674 and SNOW-678 got nothing — no comment, no linked decision doc, no "won't fix" reasoning anyone could point to later. The practical effect is identical to a page audit finding simply not being written down: the orphaned /examples/ pages and the missing regions index are exactly as true today as they were three audits ago, but nothing in Linear currently says so. A future audit that trusted ticket state instead of code — the one discipline this series exists to enforce — would report both as resolved.

/compare/ repeats the orphan pattern SNOW-668 was supposed to have generalised

Three audits running (downloads, then trips) shipped new surfaces with the reachability discipline designed in from the start, not retrofitted after this series caught a gap. This week that streak has an asterisk: /compare/ shipped reachable, then had its only inbound link deliberately removed six days later, in a commit whose own message admits the page is now unreachable. It is a smaller version of the same failure mode as SNOW-674/678 above — a page that is fine and a decision that is unrecorded — rather than a returned bug.

SNOW-937 answers "decide SNOW-7" with a different transport than every prior audit assumed

Every audit since 19 August has tracked the competitive cost of the unsent digest and recommended, in some form, deciding SNOW-7's fate. SNOW-7 is now permanently cancelled and will not be revived — but the underlying product need (tell someone when the bulletin they care about changes) is being rebuilt today as push notifications, not email, on the back of a real season of diff data rather than the two-line placeholder SNOW-7 carried since April. The recommendation this series should carry forward is no longer "decide SNOW-7"; it is "watch SNOW-937 ship, and check the delivered behaviour against docs/roadmap.md once it does."

An audit that never merges still changes what gets tracked

The 7 September audit's PR was drafted, reviewed by nobody, and closed unmerged — yet its findings drove three real tickets (SNOW-669's closure, SNOW-855, SNOW-885) via Linear comments quoting it directly. That is a good outcome for those three findings and a bad precedent for the series as a whole: this repository's own history of what was found and when now has a week-shaped hole in it, recoverable only by reading Linear comments that happen to cite an Artifact URL. See Recommendation.

Recommendation

What I'd do next

First, put a documented reason behind SNOW-674 and SNOW-678, or reopen them. Right now they are worse than open: a reader of Linear alone would have no way to know the orphaned examples pages and the missing regions index are still exactly as found. Either record why they don't fit the site's new shape (the two-documents rule is a plausible reason for SNOW-674; it is much less obviously one for SNOW-678, which only asked for a link) or put them back in the queue.

Then decide /compare/'s footer link. Cheapest possible fix in this document — restore one <a>, or write down why not — and it will be the third time in four audits this series has caught a page shipped reachable and then made unreachable with no ticket attached.

Then either merge a record of the 7 September audit or note the gap in the README. Its Artifact and closed PR are the only record that window's findings exist outside of Linear comment archaeology; a one-line README note pointing at the PR would cost almost nothing and save the next audit from having to reconstruct it, as this one did.

Then B3, still the oldest open finding in this series. docs/bulletin- guide.md has been SLF-only since before the first audit; five audits running now.

Watch SNOW-937, not SNOW-7. When A1–A4 ship, verify what actually fires against docs/roadmap.md's stated design (problem-aware diff, dispatch-on-change, NEW_BULLETIN as the default subscription mode) rather than assuming the ticket delivered what it planned.
