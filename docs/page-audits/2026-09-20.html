The short version

Terrain-aware routes is the cleanest big feature this series has audited. The three findings this series has tracked longest didn't move an inch, and the one meant to replace them stalled completely.

SNOW-909's terrain work is real and it is careful: sample_height/sample_slope (apps/locations/services/terrain.py) never return a bare None — a route with unsurveyed ground under it draws dashed grey, never green, and the map, the route panel and the Trip page all keep that distinction through to the rendered line (static/js/route_slope_core.js's UNKNOWN_COLOUR is deliberately not one of the six slope bands). The bulletin-overlap work (SNOW-839) computes a per-problem aspect/elevation match and then visibly throws the rating away before it reaches a template — ProblemOverlap.danger_rating_value is captured and never read anywhere on the display side — so "this route crosses ground the bulletin names" never becomes "this route is rated." And the "what do these marks mean" link that /compare/ and the examples pages never got is a real, working anchor here: /help/#help-topic-slope, linked from the map legend and, after SNOW-968, from the Trip page too.

Against that: this series' three oldest open findings are exactly as they were seven days ago. docs/bulletin-guide.md is now 102 days past its last review with zero ALBINA or Météo-France mentions. SNOW-674 and SNOW-678 remain Canceled with the single scoping comment each carried since 11 August and nothing since. And a fuller trace of /compare/'s footer link this week found it is not merely undocumented but deliberately permanent: the removal commit renamed its own regression test from asserting the link exists to asserting it doesn't, and it still carries no ticket.

The larger story is what didn't happen. SNOW-937 — the alerting rebuild this series has been waiting on since 19 August, and which last week's audit called "the closest thing to a real answer" — was scoped into twelve numbered child tickets (A1–A7, B1–B4) on 13 September and has had no commit, no comment and no state change on any of them since. The season-data research that justified the whole redesign is a week old and untouched. Every hour this repository's single maintainer spent this week went into routes.

N1

Terrain-aware routes (SNOW-909) shipped whole — coloured, honest about what it doesn't know, and reachable

SNOW-910, 911, 917, 951, 960–974, 988–990, 995, 839

A saved route was one flat fuchsia line until this week. It now carries a 25 m-stride slope reading of the terrain it crosses (Route.slope_samples, sampled from a 5 m elevation grid — never the track's own recorded elevation, which the decision doc calls "free, and a lie": a skin track zigzagging up a 38° face rises near-flat along its own length and would paint green under that shortcut). Six slope-angle colours, crux rings, no-fall passages and fall-line arrows render on the map overlay, the new docked route detail panel (SNOW-973), and — since SNOW-962 gave Trip its own copy of the record — the Trip page too, without re-reading the source route. Coverage gaps are a named reason (OUTSIDE_COVERAGE / NO_DATA / UNAVAILABLE), never a silent zero, and that distinction is kept by the presence of the GeoJSON slope key rather than its value, all the way to a dashed-grey line on screen. SNOW-839 scores a route against the day's bulletin by problem type, aspect and elevation band and deliberately never turns that into a rating — the computed danger_rating_value is stored and never displayed — and the shared template says so outright: "It is not a rating of your day — read the bulletin itself." The one explainer link this kind of feature needs (/help/#help-topic-slope) is real, resolves, and is linked from three places, not zero.

Consequences

What this week's shape changes about the older tickets

Connections between this cycle's decisions and tickets written before them — visible from neither side alone.

"Watch SNOW-937 ship" was last week's recommendation; this week answers whether it's still moving, and it isn't

Every audit since 19 August has tracked the cost of the unsent alert. Last week reframed the recommendation around SNOW-937 as a real, data-backed redesign rather than the two-line SNOW-7 placeholder it replaced. This week that redesign didn't advance by one commit: twelve child tickets exist, all unstarted, and the parent has no comment newer than the day it was scoped. That is not necessarily a problem — a maintainer working alone chose to spend the week on terrain-aware routes instead, and that work was substantial and well-executed — but it is not visible anywhere in Linear as a choice. A ticket marked "In Progress, High priority" that has had zero activity for a week reads identically whether it was deliberately paused or simply forgotten, and only Hugo knows which.

/compare/'s missing link is not an open question any more — the test suite already settled it

Last week's recommendation was to "decide /compare/'s footer link" as the cheapest open item in the document. This week's deeper trace found the decision was already made, in writing, in code: PR #893 didn't just remove the link, it renamed the regression test that used to assert it existed into one that asserts it doesn't. That is a stronger signal of intent than most tickets in this codebase get. The gap this audit should now track is narrower and cheaper to close than "restore or decide" — it's simply that the decision has no home a future reader of Linear or docs/decisions/ would ever find, unlike every other deliberate policy in this codebase.

A second reader-facing explainer is arriving before the first one's reachability question is answered

docs/explainers/from-gpx-to-line.html shipped this week with the same shape of gap this series flagged in /compare/ and the examples pages: a real page, honestly written, reachable by nobody clicking anything on the live site. The difference is that this time the next ticket in the queue (SNOW-977, in progress) already names the same question explicitly for its own companion page, rather than this audit having to surface it after the fact. That is a genuine improvement in how this failure mode gets caught — but it also means two documents in this new series are now live with two different unresolved homes (docs/explainers/, publicly framed; docs/route-anatomy/, an open question whether it's public at all), and answering the question once, for the series, would be cheaper than answering it twice.

Terrain-aware routes is the counter-example this series has been waiting for

Three audits running, this series has repeated a pattern: a new surface ships reachable and honest, then loses its only link with no ticket (/compare/), or a tracked reachability finding gets cancelled without being fixed (SNOW-674, SNOW-678). SNOW-909's terrain work is the first big feature in this run of audits that got the hard parts right from the start — the unknown-state handling, the no-rating discipline on the bulletin overlap, the working help link — without a page audit having to catch and report the gap first. It is worth naming as the standard the two long-cancelled tickets are still failing to meet, not because the two are related in code, but because both are now live evidence of what this team can do when reachability is treated as part of the ticket rather than an audit's job to notice.

Recommendation

What I'd do next

First, put a documented reason behind SNOW-674 and SNOW-678, or reopen them. Two audits and two weeks running with a Canceled status and zero explanatory comment. The cost of writing one sentence on each ticket is smaller every week it isn't paid.

Then write down /compare/'s policy, not its fix. This is no longer "decide whether to relink it" — PR #893 already decided, and encoded the decision as a test. What's missing is one comment on a ticket, or a line in docs/decisions/, saying a sitemap-only page is the intended shape. That is minutes of work against three audits of this series flagging the same page.

Then confirm whether SNOW-937 is paused on purpose. If routes were the right call for the week, say so on the ticket so the next audit doesn't have to guess between "deliberately reprioritised" and "quietly dropped" — they read identically from outside Linear.

Then B3, still the oldest open finding in this series — docs/bulletin- guide.md has been SLF-only since before the first audit, six audits running now.

Cheapest of all: decide the public-explainer reachability question once. from-gpx-to-line.html is live, and SNOW-977's companion piece asks the identical question mid-ticket. One decision — link from /help/, or keep the series developer- facing and say so in CLAUDE.md's own description — settles both rather than one at a time.
