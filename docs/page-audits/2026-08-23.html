Where Snowdesk's pages stand, twelve days on

A re-run of the 11 August audit against current main. Four findings closed, ten unchanged, and one new architectural direction that reshapes the biggest ticket in the set.

The short version

The urgent things shipped. The unsent email did not.

Both items flagged as most urgent are done. The legal pages now cover all three providers, and they carry the exact reconciliation this document proposed — a section headed "A complete rendering, not the authority". The account page has been split into a hub and a settings page, and the button that read "Unsubscribe from all alerts" while deleting the entire account now says what it does.

The headline finding is untouched. There is still no digest command, the scheduler still runs two jobs, and all three surfaces still promise a daily email that nothing sends. It has also acquired a competitor. The scan added on 19 August names AvalancheClarity as a new entrant that already ships push-on-new-bulletin — recorded in docs/competitors.md as "the idea we flagged as our strongest available differentiator, already shipped by someone else". The gap is no longer only a truthfulness problem.

Meanwhile the product grew in a direction the audit did not anticipate: GPX routes, a slope-angle overlay, and a new architectural spine — Location as the domain primitive — accepted, corrected and superseded within a single day.

New

What the audit didn't anticipate

N1

Location is now the domain primitive

9 tickets

A model direction proposed, accepted and superseded on the same day — the second doc withdrawing §4–5 of the first because "the arrow was backwards", having checked the privacy justification against the code and found it unsupported. The conclusion: one Location table in a new apps/locations/, which every place FKs to — resort, mid-station, peak, favourite, observation, region centroid — with ForecastPoint renamed ForecastCell to say it is a quantised fetch cell rather than a domain entity.

Not built yet: apps/locations/ does not exist and ForecastCell is not defined. SNOW-700 through SNOW-709 carry it.

  • Reasoning worth keeping: "quantisation is a cost mechanism that was mistaken for a privacy boundary, and a model was built on the mistake"
N2

GPX routes shipped as a map surface, with no page of its own

apps/routes/

A whole new app: upload, parse, list, rename, delete, draw on the map, elevation profile in the popup, start and finish markers. Its templates are six partials and no full page, so it follows the favourites pattern — which is also how favourites ended up with a detail page and no index. Worth deciding deliberately this time whether saved routes need a home in the account area, rather than discovering the gap in a later audit.

N3

The competitive picture changed under the unsent email

docs/competitors.md

The 19 August scan added AvalancheClarity — a free app that plain-language-explains the Météo-France bulletin, works offline, and pushes on new bulletins and revisions for a subscribed region. The doc is candid that this is Snowdesk's own flagged differentiator, already shipped elsewhere, and notes that /_push-demo/ remains the only thing in this codebase that has ever sent a push.

Consequences

Three things the new work changes about the old tickets

These are connections between the last twelve days' decisions and tickets written before them. None is visible from either side alone.

SNOW-680 is no longer blocked

It was parked on one question: which forecast point represents a region with no resorts? SNOW-696 answers it — the region centroid, anchored as a Location, reaching weather through location.forecast_cell, so the bulletin page inherits the same multi-day panel the resort page already has. The local-knowledge layer can move to Ready for dev once that lands.

SNOW-665 should be re-scoped, not implemented as written

It proposes a place model with a PIN/REGION discriminator and conditionally-nullable coordinates. Under location-is-the-primitive that shape is obsolete before it is built: a saved thing references a Location, and once SNOW-696 gives every micro-region a centroid Location, both kinds resolve the same way — which dissolves the discriminator and both of the open questions the ticket is parked on. Re-scope it against SNOW-700 rather than migrating twice, exactly as SNOW-696 chose to do.

The account area is half-built, and that is the visible half

SNOW-667 delivered the hub and settings, which are the plumbing. The two pages a user actually came for — /account/places/ and /account/observations/ — are still Todo and Ready respectively, and SNOW-705 ("design the account-area navigation and page layouts") is open behind them. Until those land, the account area is a settings screen with a hub in front of it.

Positioning

The reframe held, and is now in the product

This isn't a simplified bulletin with data missing. It is the real bulletin — you can follow the source link and find the same data. What Snowdesk adds is what the provider can't: weather at a finer grain than the warning region, and what people actually found on the ground there.

This is no longer only a working principle. /terms/ now says it in public, under the heading "A complete rendering, not the authority".

The three tickets that make the claim true, however, are all still open — SNOW-670 (render the off-schedule flag, the one field known to break the claim), SNOW-671 (the guard that would stop it breaking again) and SNOW-681 (the verification link the claim rests on). The public promise landed ahead of the engineering that backs it. That is the sharpest thing in this re-run, and it is a reason to move SNOW-670 and SNOW-671 up rather than a reason to soften the terms page.

Recommendation

What I'd do next

First, SNOW-670 and SNOW-671. Both are Ready, both are small, and together they close the gap between what /terms/ now claims and what the page actually renders. Shipping a public completeness claim while a known field goes unrendered is the one thing here I would not leave open another twelve days.

Then decide the email, properly. Not the implementation — the commitment. A competitor now ships the alert; the copy still promises it; the ticket reads as a feature. Either it moves up and gets built, or the three copy surfaces get corrected in the meantime so the product stops claiming something it doesn't do. The one option that isn't available is another twelve days of both.

Then re-scope SNOW-665 against SNOW-700 before anyone picks it up, and move SNOW-680 to Ready once SNOW-696 lands. Both are bookkeeping, and both prevent rework.

The cheap wins remain cheap and remain unclaimed: SNOW-669, SNOW-676 and SNOW-678 are each a couple of hours and between them end every orphan in the original audit.
