Bulletin change analysis · CH-4115 Martigny / Verbier

What actually changes
in a bulletin

Snowdesk is about to start sending people a notification when the avalanche bulletin for a place they follow changes. Before writing that notification, it is worth knowing what a bulletin change actually is. One region, one full season, every issue: 296 bulletins across 189 days.

2025-11-01 → 2026-05-18 296 bulletins · 189 days · 187 transitions source: SLF SNOW-937
The finding

13.4%

The share of days on which the headline danger rating moved at all. On the other 86%, a notification keyed to the danger rating has nothing to say — and on two days in three of those it would be wrong to say nothing, because something in the bulletin did change.

02 · The three messages

Every day falls into one of three buckets, and the smallest is the one we were going to build for

The 25 rating moves split almost evenly: 13 up, 12 down. Every one of them was a single step — moderate↔considerable (16), considerable↔high (6), low↔moderate (3). No jump of two levels occurred all season.

03 · Inside the 107

What moves when the rating doesn't

Two things dominate, and neither is a number the reader would see on a danger scale. A problem keeps its name and moves its geometry — the height it starts at, the slopes it applies to. That is the single most common event in a season, and it is exactly the kind of change that decides whether a planned route is affected.

Wind slab is the volatile one — 38 of the 68 arrivals and departures. Persistent weak layers, by contrast, were named on 166 of 189 days. A problem present 88% of the season carries almost no notification value; its arrival is not news, and neither is its persistence.

04 · The second issue

SLF publishes twice a day. The second one usually says nothing new

107 of the 189 days carried two issues — an evening bulletin around 16:00 UTC and a morning update around 07:00. The obvious design is to send on both. The data says otherwise.

The rating-move rate holds between 10% and 22% in every single month — there is no part of the season where the bulletin settles down and no part where it goes wild. The highest is May (22%), a shoulder month, when the fewest people are watching.

Quiet stretches are short. The 55 no-change days fall into 35 separate runs, and the longest run all season is four days — so long as a bulletin was published at all. The one real silence of the season is the coverage gap: no bulletin was issued between 6 and 15 November, eleven days on which a change-driven notification would have had nothing to send.

06 · Consequences

Four things this changes about what gets built

SNOW-938 · A1

The diff has to read problems, not ratings

A change service that compares danger ratings is silent on 86% of days and wrong on 57% of them. It has to compare the avalanche problem set and each problem's elevation band and aspect sector. Those live in Bulletin.render_model["traits"][*]["problems"] — not in RegionDayRating, whose bands field is ALBINA-only and None for every Swiss row.

A3

The payload needs a vocabulary for geometry

“Risk level increased” covers 25 days of the season. The other 107 need sentences about a problem arriving, a problem clearing, or a band moving — wind slab now north through east above 2400. That is the same vocabulary docs/day-summary.md already defines; it should not be reinvented.

A4

Fire on change, not on a clock

A daily cron at a fixed hour misses the morning update; a twice-daily cron is noise 87% of the time it fires. Running on the existing hourly fetch_bulletins cadence and sending only when the authoritative bulletin for the target day actually differs gives roughly one send a day, catches the 14 meaningful morning updates, and handles the season's single unscheduled re-issue without a special case.

SNOW-939 · A2

“Only on change” is the safe default

The longest silence a change-driven feed would have produced all season is four days. The argument for a daily heartbeat was that people forget a channel that goes quiet — four days is not long enough for that to bite, and Web Push permission, once revoked, cannot be asked for again.

07 · Method

How this was counted, and what it does not cover

Every line of apps/bulletins/local_mirrors/slf_archive.ndjson whose regions list contains CH-4115 — 296 records. Each was assigned to a target day by the end of its validTime, giving 189 days with one gap in coverage. Where a day carried several issues, the one with the latest publicationTime was treated as authoritative, matching the rule RegionDayRating already uses. Consecutive days were then compared on headline rating, Swiss sub-rating, problem set, each problem's elevation bounds and aspect list, and the set of validity periods.

Three limits worth stating plainly. This is one region of one provider: Météo-France and ALBINA archives are committed alongside it and have not been checked, and their publication rhythms differ. CH-4115 is a high, glaciated region whose season runs November to May — a lower region would show more no-rating days and a shorter season. And a season is one sample: 2025/26 was a persistent-weak-layer winter, which is why that problem sits on 88% of days, and a different winter would redistribute the problem counts even if the shape of the finding held.

The danger-scale colours above are the EAWS specification values, used unchanged because the standard mandates them. Every other colour on this page was validated for colour-vision separation against both the light and dark surface.
