# Competitor landscape — 2026-11-12

Window: scans of 2026-08-19 to 2026-11-08, about twelve weekly passes over
the seven profiled products and the "Not yet profiled" table in
[`competitors.md`](../../competitors.md). Method:
[README](README.md). First edition, so no trend against a previous one.

## Summary

Bulletin access has become a commodity. Competitors now differentiate on
three things: **alerts**, **terrain tools next to the bulletin** and
**distribution through other people's products**. Nobody has built a
normalised cross-provider render, or route-versus-today's-bulletin coupling
across all providers. Little money is visible, and nobody earns it from the
bulletin itself.

## Caveats first

- **Off-season window.** Mid-August to early November is before the Alpine
  season opens, so product cadence is understated. Real releases cluster in
  October and November: Whympr 3.0 (9 Oct), SnowSafe 6, OpenSnow's 2026-27
  release (30 Oct).
- **Search-based, mostly unverified.** Many notes come from search snippets,
  not the product's own page. A prior SnowSafe reading ("no Switzerland or
  France") was reversed on 2026-10-11, and Avycast's coverage figures
  disagree across sources.
- **Silence is not evidence.** "No funding news" means none surfaced in
  search. Whympr's funding totals conflict across aggregators and none is
  dated 2026.
- **Selection effect.** Some new entrants (Avycast, Global Avy, Granite)
  may have existed before the scans looked for them.

## Patterns

### 1. Feature development dominates, and it converges

| Feature | Products doing it | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Push alerts on a new bulletin or a danger threshold | SnowSafe (v6.3), AvalancheClarity, Global Avy, Land Tirol, OpenSnow (snowfall thresholds), Diedamskopf (geofenced, no app) | Strong |
| Slope-angle or avalanche-terrain layer | WhiteRisk, Whympr, onX (ATES), Varsom, Bergfex PRO, OpenSnow (free, 30 Oct) | Strong |
| Bulletin or risk shown against a route | WhiteRisk (cruxes), Yéti/Skitourenguru, PeakVisor, Skida v2.0, Trail Connect, Visorando | Strong |
| Aspect and elevation dial | SnowSafe, Skida, OpenSnow (aspect overlay) | Moderate |
| Danger history or observations | PeakVisor (7-day history, translated reports), WhiteRisk (crowd reports), Whympr (90-day expiry), Avy, Utah, Tirol SNOBS | Moderate |

### 2. Cross-provider coverage is cheap, so it is a weak moat

SnowSafe lists 25 services in 12 countries. AvalancheClarity, a one-person
project, covers 14 countries. Avycast went from 21 to 30+ regions and added a
web version. WhiteRisk added the French bulletin and its terrain maps in
France and Austria. Skida consolidates several providers in one app. Most of
this is pass-through links, not a normalised render. Depth, not breadth, is
what remains open.

### 3. Acquisition is moving to embedding and licensing

- AvalancheClarity's widget targets resorts, guides and chalet companies.
- Outdooractive licenses Skitourenguru's terrain-hazard layer.
- SnowSafe licenses its avalanche and weather data through a business API.
- Trail Connect and Visorando embed the French bulletin in route apps.
- NWAC's Avy app has been joined by seven other US avalanche centres, which
  split the cost.
- Whympr's 27 Oct post courts ex-FATMAP users.

### 4. New segments are reached by language, beginners and adjacency

- **Language:** AI translation in AvalancheClarity (8 languages), Avycast
  (11) and SnowSafe (15).
- **Non-experts:** the EAWS survey on new danger-scale wording aims at
  quicker comprehension, especially for Level 3.
- **Adjacent users:** OpenSnow is moving from powder-chasers toward
  avalanche planning, and Whympr toward hikers (trail difficulty, hunting
  zones, water sources). Resorts are starting to send alerts themselves.

### 5. Money and funding are quiet

- No acquisitions and no team moves worth recording.
- Visible funding: Skida's Folkeinvest crowdfunding round, AspectAvy's
  Wefunder raise and the Petzl Foundation's funding of Yéti.
- Many players are solo or tiny: AvalancheClarity, Avycast, Global Avy,
  Skida (about 4 people), OpenSnow ("small team").
- Monetisation is split:

  | Model | Examples |
  |---|---|
  | Free, no ads, no in-app purchases | AvalancheClarity |
  | Free, ad-supported | SnowSafe |
  | Freemium with paid terrain, routes or weather | Whympr, Skida, Bergfex PRO, Visorando |
  | Rising subscription with safety features given away | OpenSnow (Base $49.99, Premium $99.99; slope and aspect free) |

### 6. Official providers are becoming app makers

WhiteRisk, NWAC, the Utah Avalanche Center, Avalanche Canada (rebuild),
Varsom and Land Tirol all own their delivery channel. That squeezes
third-party apps from the supply side.

## State of the category

Crowded at the bottom, converging in the middle, open at the top.

- **Bottom:** anyone can ship a multi-country bulletin reader.
- **Middle:** alerts and slope or aspect layers are becoming table stakes,
  even for forecast-led apps.
- **Top (open):** a normalised cross-provider render, and route-versus-
  today's-bulletin coupling across all providers. Yéti and Skitourenguru come
  closest, but cover the French Alps and a Swiss-rooted database.
- **Business model:** none found for the bulletin itself. Revenue comes from
  weather, route planning, maps or licensing.

## Read-across to Snowdesk

- **Alerts (SNOW-838, Feature inspiration item 1):** the pattern is
  strongest here and the differentiation is gone. The reference designs are
  SnowSafe (threshold plus new-bulletin toggle) and Global Avy
  (de-duplication, opt-in prompt).
- **Route coupling (item 2):** withdrawn on our side while six unrelated
  products ship a version. Worth revisiting once the daily problem-terrain
  layer (SNOW-979) has settled.
- **Distribution:** embedding and licensing is the unexplored channel for us.
  AvalancheClarity's widget and Outdooractive's licence are the precedents.
- **Open lane:** normalised rendering across CH, FR, AT and IT remains ours
  until someone ships it.
