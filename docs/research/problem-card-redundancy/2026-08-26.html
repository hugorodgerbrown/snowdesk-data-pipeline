Four Ways
to Say Dry

Every problem card states its category four times — in the panel title, the EAWS pictogram, the problem label, and a DRY / WET pill. Across 9,614 traits in production the pill has never once been the only one speaking. The title stays; the pill goes. The ALL DAY pill goes with it on the 96% of pages where the day never splits — and the card edge takes on the saturated EAWS colour that today gets four pixels above the band.

What the sweep measured

Four figures
100% Every card already names its category in the title

9,614 of 9,614 traits carry a title, and all 17 distinct title strings name the category. The fallback at render_model.py:1604 fills whatever the provider leaves blank. The title is staying — which is precisely what makes the pill removable.

96.0% Pages where the day never splits

7,543 of 7,855 bulletins are all_day throughout — 100% of Météo-France, 91.7% of SLF, 87.1% of ALBINA. On every one of them the time pill repeats one fact on every surface that can hold it.

79.6% Pages carrying exactly one card

6,255 of 7,855. With one card on the page there is nothing to tell apart, so both pills spend their space labelling a set of one.

4px All the saturated EAWS colour the card gets today

A stripe on the band's top edge (main.css:623), sitting below the title row so it reads as the band's decoration rather than the card's identity. The level is the most scannable thing on the page and it is spending four pixels.

And in dark mode, the two pills are barely legible

The EAWS scale is mandated by the standard, so .dark in main.css overrides none of it — the danger band keeps its light tint on a dark card, deliberately. But SNOW-286 changed the category and time chips from filled pills to transparent ghosts, and a ghost inherits --color-text-2, which dark mode brightens to #a8a49c. Light-grey text, pale tint, no effective border: translucent white at 16% over #ffe5c2 is not a border.

Chosen. Reads level, then problem, then scope, with the saturation falling away as you go — which is what makes a page of stacked cards scannable. The accepted cost is that the title bar states the category, so the strongest colour lands on the row about the other axis; the hazard mark, which encodes both, keeps them tied together.

Both roles come from pairings the palette already ships

The scale carries four values per level: the solid, the -tint, -fg for copy on the solid, and -text for copy on the tint. C and D are the same card with the roles swapped, and at levels 1–3 they use those two pairings exactly as designed — no new token, no invention.

Only the top two levels need thought, and neither needs a new value. EAWS red takes no companion that clears AA — white is 4.00:1, the level’s own dark #6b0000 is 3.23:1. So the saturated role borrows the other dark value the level already owns: at High, -text #6b0000 becomes the surface with white copy; at Very high the solid stays and its own tint #1a0000 becomes the copy. That also gives 4 and 5 visibly different cards, which no unmodified treatment manages — the standard assigns them one hue.

The seam holds at every level. Luminance contrast between the two rows is low at Low and Moderate (1.08:1 and 1.02:1), which looks alarming written down. It is the wrong measure for two large blocks of colour: perceptual distance is ΔE 39.6 and 64.9, far above the ~10 at which a boundary reads as a boundary. Both rows are normal text at 12px/600 and 15px/600, so the 4.5:1 floor in the table is the one that governs.

Whichever wins, it replaces the level stripe. Keeping both puts the same colour on two surfaces a few pixels apart. The stripe’s stated job in main.css is “an unmistakable level identifier even at a glance”; a filled row does that from a surface the eye already lands on.

The change

One kept, four cut, one moved
Keep The panel title, exactly where it is

card.panel_title stays its own row inside _rating_block.html, unchanged. It is what makes the category pill removable, and it is the surface the fidelity probe reads the category from.

Cut The DRY / WET pill

Remove the card.category branch from _rating_block.html and the type-dry / type-wet variants from _chip.html. Category survives in the title, the pictogram and the label.

Cut The per-card time pill, unless the day splits

Gate it on is_time_variable, which already exists at views.py:6090 and is already in the panel context. It disappears from 7,543 pages and stays on all 312 that split. The day-risk-profile pill stays unconditional.

Cut The level-number chip

The bare digit restates the tint, the pictogram and the new edge. Its suffix does not: 3− is SLF's subdivision, and it has no other home on the page. Give it one in the day-risk row as words — Considerable, lower end — which reads better than a glyph most users will not decode. This is the one edit here that moves data rather than removing decoration; see the guard note below.

Move The saturated level colour, from the stripe to the title bar

Delete the five border-top: 4px rules at main.css:623–627 and fill the title bar instead, keyed on a data-level attribute the row does not yet carry — _rating_block.html will need to stamp it. Levels 1–3 take --color-eaws-{level} with -fg copy. Levels 4 and 5 borrow the other dark value each already owns: High takes -text #6b0000 as its surface with white copy, and Very high keeps the solid with its own tint #1a0000 as copy. No new token. The hazard bar is unchanged — it already renders tint plus -text.

Check The title bar is now load-bearing, so its guard matters

_rating_block.html wraps the row in {% if card.panel_title %}, and its own docstring still says panel_title is “empty for ALBINA and MeteoFrance”. That was true of the old aggregation-driven path; it is not true of the render-model traits the page serves today, where all 9,614 carry a title. Under D a missing title bar would take the level colour with it, so either drop the conditional or give it an explicit fallback — and fix the docstring either way.

Sweep Four tokens that were already dead

--color-type-dry-bg and its three siblings (main.css:158, :565) are defined, documented in the component library at design_tokens.py:300, and consumed by nothing. SNOW-286 changed the pill to a transparent ghost and left the fill colours behind.

What the guards will say

fidelity-lint · ds-lint

Keeping the title makes the fidelity story trivial

customData.CH.aggregation[].category (fidelity.py:552) is declared rendered with the note “Dry/wet category pill on the trait block”. Its literal() probe needs the word dry or wet in the page text — which the title supplies on all 9,614 traits. Only the note's wording changes.

avalancheProblems[].validTimePeriod (fidelity.py:549) uses snake_label(), so all day must appear in the rendered text. That is why the day-risk-profile pill stays unconditional — drop it too and every all-day sentinel fails.

ds-lint is unaffected: the border comes from data-level attribute selectors in main.css, the same mechanism the stripe uses today, so no raw palette utility or arbitrary radius reaches a template.

Cutting the level-number chip is the one change that breaks a guard

Two rows depend on it — avalancheProblems[].customData.CH.subdivision (fidelity.py:530) and dangerRatings[].customData.CH.subdivision (fidelity.py:566). The second reads “Level-number chip in the Day Risk Profile”, but its subdivision_suffix() probe is a regex anchored on data-testid="level-number-chip" — a testid only _rating_block.html emits. The day-windows tile renders the same string with no testid and aria-hidden="true". So both rows are in fact satisfied by the card chip alone, and removing it fails tox -e test twice over.

The fix is not to move the testid onto an aria-hidden tile. Render the subdivision as words in the day-risk row and rewrite the probe to match those words — it is the surface the second declaration already claims, and a written lower end is legible where 3− is not. Budget the ticket for a probe change, not just a template one.

Figures from the production database, 26 Aug 2026 — 7,855 bulletins carrying at least one trait, 9,614 traits, read from render_model→traits rather than the raw provider payloads. The distinction matters: the raw SLF feed leaves 60% of aggregation titles empty, and render_model.py:1604 fills every one of them before the page ever sees it. Which way the 312 go

Addendum · 27 Aug 2026

The sweep above counted the days that split and stopped there, because the question was whether a time pill distinguishes anything. It does not answer the next one: when the day does split, which way does it go? This addendum decomposes the same 312 bulletins by direction, to ground the Day Risk Profile's second row rather than the card's chip rail.

It reaches those 312 from the other side of the page. The sweep read trait periods, the surface is_time_variable reads (views.py:6090); this reads dangerRatings[].validTimePeriod, the surface compute_period_transition reads (render_model.py:2388). Across the whole archive the two never disagree — 312 bulletins split on both, 7,768 split on neither, and no bulletin splits on one alone. The profile row and the card pills appear and vanish together.

60.6% Split days that deteriorate

189 of 312 — 2.3% of the archive. Deterioration outnumbers improvement 8.6:1, so the second row is, in practice, a row about the day getting worse.

32.4% Split days where the level does not move

101 of 312 carry a split with the same level on both sides — direction="none", the flat-but-split case. The two rows read identically and the whole of the news is in the problem changing underneath them.

7.1% Split days that improve

22 of 312, 0.3% of the archive, and only 10 of them move a whole level. Any treatment built specifically for an improving day is being designed for ten pages a season.

0 Falling days outside SLF

ALBINA splits 127 times and never once goes down. Météo-France carries no validTimePeriod on its ratings at all, so its 4,671 bulletins cannot split. Every improving day in the archive is Swiss.

A third of the second rows say nothing the first row did not

direction="none" is 101 bulletins — nearly five times commoner than a whole-level improvement, and the case with no number to show. Both rows carry the same digit, the same word and the same colour, and the reason the row exists at all is a problem that changes underneath it. Rendered as two identical rows it reads as a duplication bug rather than a finding, which is the failure mode the specimen in shape C above hides by happening to be a rising day.

The component's copy is worth sizing to the same distribution. Rising is 61% of the second rows and falling is 7%; a symmetric up/down treatment spends equal design on a case the archive produces ten times a season. The row that needs the words is the one where the level does not move at all.
